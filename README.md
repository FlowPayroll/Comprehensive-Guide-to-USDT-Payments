## Receive USDT: a basic workflow

1. **Choose a compatible wallet or custodial account.** Confirm that it supports the exact USDT asset and blockchain you intend to use.
2. **Open the receive/deposit screen.** Select USDT and its designated network, then obtain the displayed address or QR code.
3. **Agree on the blockchain with the sender.** A familiar-looking address alone does not prove network compatibility.
4. **Share the address securely.** Copy it or use a QR code, and check that it has not been altered.
5. **Verify the incoming transaction.** Check the transaction status and the recipient account's required confirmations or crediting rules.
6. **Reconcile the payment.** Compare the actual credited amount with the invoice, accounting for any fees charged by intermediaries.

## Why USDT is used for international payments

A stablecoin transfer can provide an alternative to some conventional cross-border payment routes. Depending on the providers and networks involved, it may offer accessible transfer rails or convenient settlement. However, the cost, speed, availability, and ability to convert funds locally vary widely. Stablecoins are not exempt from fraud, market, compliance, or tax considerations.

## Compare the networks

| USDT network | Underlying blockchain | Token typically needed for network fees | Key consideration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TRC20** | Tron | TRX or available Tron network resources | Fee model depends on account resources and current network conditions |
| **BEP20** | BNB Smart Chain | BNB | Often inexpensive, but support must be confirmed |
| **ERC20** | Ethereum | ETH | Gas costs fluctuate with network demand |

**Always match the deposit network to the sending network.** BEP20 and ERC20 addresses may both start with `0x`, but this does not make them interchangeable. Transfers made on unsupported networks may be difficult or impossible to recover.

See **[Chapter 1](chapters/01-networks.md)** for the detailed comparison.

## Understand all payment costs

The price of a transaction depends on more than the blockchain's fee. Check network gas or resources, provider withdrawal/deposit charges, trading commissions, exchange-rate spreads, and bank or off-ramp charges. A self-custody wallet does not generally charge a separate on-chain receipt fee, but custodial providers may have their own policies.

Before proceeding, compare the **total amount the recipient will actually receive**, not just the advertised network fee.

## Essential security practices

- Confirm the blockchain and token type with the recipient before sending.
- Verify the full address or use a trusted, authenticated address-book entry; remain alert to clipboard manipulation and address-poisoning scams.
- For unfamiliar destinations and significant payments, consider an affordable test transfer.
- Store wallet recovery phrases offline and never disclose them to support agents or other parties.
- Review the recipient and transaction details carefully: completed blockchain transfers generally cannot be reversed.
